## Task

Work in a small group to propose four broad professional skills and four role-specific skills important to a data analyst or data engineer. Explain the choices to the cohort, then refine the combined suggestions into a shared model.

The challenge was to prioritise and justify the choices, rather than list every potentially useful skill.

## My contribution

I presented my group's ideas during the feedback session. The broad skills I reported were:

- Effective communication, including verbal and written communication.
- Problem solving.
- Continuous learning as technologies change.
- Collaboration and teamwork.

For role-specific skills, I reported **Python, SQL, Excel and cloud**. I also raised presentation skills; the trainer placed these with the broader professional skills during the discussion.

Later, I suggested **Tableau and Excel** as examples of visualisation tools.

## How the cohort refined the ideas

The trainer collected the groups' proposals, identified overlap and invited explanations for the choices. The discussion moved from named technologies to the wider capabilities they support. Continuous learning was considered within adaptability, and critical thinking within problem solving.

Our group's wording, **effective communication**, was explicitly picked up in the discussion. The cohort explored what makes communication effective, including clarity, relevance, concision and suitability for the audience.

## Shared outcome

| Broad professional skills | Technical depth |
| :--- | :--- |
| Effective communication | Programming proficiency |
| Collaboration | Query languages |
| Proactive problem solving | Cloud computing |
| Adaptability | Fundamental data concepts |

The two columns are separate lists, not one-to-one mappings. The technical category of fundamental data concepts brought together ideas such as data modelling, quality and visualisation.

## Learning from the exercise

Grouping related ideas makes a skills model easier to explain. It also helps distinguish a durable capability, such as programming or communicating findings, from a particular tool used to apply it.

For future project documentation, this means showing what a tool helped achieve and why the approach was appropriate. Naming Python or Excel alone says less than explaining the problem solved with it.
