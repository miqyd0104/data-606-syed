## Professional expectations

Training forms part of my employment as a Junior Data Consultant at Sparta Global. Its purpose is to develop both practical data skills and the ability to work effectively with colleagues and clients. Being able to explain an approach and demonstrate reliable working habits matters alongside producing a correct result.

The session described four areas used to assess development throughout training:

| Area | What it means in practice |
| :--- | :--- |
| Delivery | Complete the work to the required standard and meet agreed deadlines. |
| Engagement | Participate, ask useful questions, contribute answers and take an active interest in the work. |
| Professionalism | Be punctual, respectful and reliable; communicate concerns and respond constructively to feedback. |
| Communication | Explain technical and business ideas clearly, in writing and when speaking, in a way suited to the audience. |

Assessment is broader than a test score. Regular tasks, group work, presentations and everyday behaviour provide evidence over time. Feedback should lead to a visible change in how the next task is approached.

Professional presentation was also discussed: prepare for meetings, use an appropriate profile image and dress for the working context. Specific workplace requirements should be checked against the relevant guidance and expectations of the team.

**Portfolio implication:** A useful project record explains the task, my contribution, the approach, the checks performed and the result. This gives a reviewer something concrete to assess.

## Solving problems and asking for help

The session introduced **three before me** as a way to build independence when attempting a task:

1. **Try it myself.** Read the brief, break the problem into smaller steps and make an initial attempt.
2. **Research the issue.** Use relevant documentation and other learning resources to investigate the part that is unclear.
3. **Ask a colleague.** Compare understanding or approaches with someone working on a similar task.
4. **Escalate when still blocked.** Explain the problem to the trainer, including what I have already tried.

There is an important distinction between uncertainty about **the task itself** and difficulty **solving the task**. If the instructions or expected outcome are unclear, ask the person who set the work for clarification directly.

A practical way to apply this when requesting help is to state the expected result, the actual result, the attempts made and the specific point where support is needed.

## Communication and daily working practices

### Effective communication

The group moved beyond describing communication as simply “good”. Effective communication should make it easier for the other person to understand the point and act on it.

| Quality | Practical meaning |
| :--- | :--- |
| Clear | Use language the audience understands and avoid ambiguous requests. |
| Concise | Include enough detail to convey the message without unnecessary repetition. |
| Relevant | Focus on the information needed for the decision or task. |
| Engaging | Help the audience follow the explanation and understand why it matters. |
| Appropriate to the audience | Adjust technical depth, examples and format to the people receiving the message. |

Being concise does not mean leaving out important information. A technical explanation may need detail, but that detail should support the purpose of the conversation.

Presentations can take different forms: slides, a diagram, a demonstration or a walkthrough of a GitHub README. The format should serve the audience and the message. The same work may need a different explanation for a technical colleague and a business stakeholder.

### Working as part of a team

The session emphasised being ready for scheduled calls, staying contactable during working time, listening to others and raising blockers early. Slack was introduced as the main messaging channel for the cohort, with Teams used for calls. The task board provided a place to find work and supporting resources.

Stand-ups were introduced as short updates about:

- What has been done since the previous update.
- What is planned next.
- Any blockers or support needed.

Detailed problem-solving conversations can follow separately so that the update remains useful to the whole team. The training version was described as an adaptation of this practice, rather than a complete lesson on Scrum.

## The T-shaped professional

A T-shaped professional combines **breadth across workplace skills** with **depth in a specialist area**. For a data consultant, technical ability needs to sit alongside communication, collaboration and the ability to adapt to different working environments.

The cohort first brainstormed skills in small groups, then combined overlapping ideas into four broad skills and four areas of technical depth.

### Broad professional skills

| Agreed skill | Meaning discussed in the session |
| :--- | :--- |
| Effective communication | Explain ideas clearly and concisely, with relevant detail for the audience. |
| Collaboration | Work constructively with others, coordinate tasks and contribute to shared outcomes. |
| Proactive problem solving | Take initiative, investigate problems and use critical thinking to work towards a solution. |
| Adaptability | Respond to changing tools, requirements and contexts through continuous learning. |

Other ideas included business understanding, time management, presentation skills and ways of working such as Agile. These remained useful considerations; the exercise grouped related ideas to keep the final model focused.

### Technical depth

| Agreed area | Examples discussed |
| :--- | :--- |
| Programming proficiency | Using languages such as Python to express logic, automate work and process data. |
| Query languages | Retrieving and working with data through queries, including SQL. |
| Cloud computing | Understanding how to work with data and infrastructure beyond a local machine. |
| Fundamental data concepts | Data quality, sources, types, formats, modelling and visualisation. |

**Concepts over tools** was a central theme. Python is one way to apply programming skills; Excel, Tableau and Power BI are examples of tools used to communicate data visually. Understanding the underlying purpose makes it easier to learn a different tool or work with an unfamiliar technology stack.

The four technical areas describe capabilities to develop. They are not a claim that all of them were taught or mastered on Day 1.

## Data roles and the purpose of engineering

The session introduced three overlapping areas of data work:

| Role | Main focus introduced |
| :--- | :--- |
| Data engineer | Move, prepare and manage data so that it is available in an appropriate form for downstream use. |
| Data analyst | Explore data and communicate findings through analysis, reports and visualisations. |
| Data scientist | Apply statistical and modelling approaches, including predictive analysis and machine learning. |

These are introductory distinctions. Responsibilities overlap, and a job title alone does not fully describe the work. The presentation exercise was set to investigate these differences further.

### Data pipelines and ETL

Data engineering was described as getting data to the right place **in the right form**. Moving data alone is insufficient if it is unsuitable for its intended use.

- **ETL — Extract, Transform, Load:** obtain data from a source, change or prepare it, then load it into a destination.
- **ELT — Extract, Load, Transform:** obtain data, load it into the destination environment, then carry out transformations there.

The course overview connected these ideas to Python, APIs, databases and cloud services. They were introduced as future areas of practical work; no pipeline implementation was completed in these sessions.

Visualisation also needs a purpose. The useful question is whether a chart helps its audience understand the finding, rather than whether a chart can be produced from the data.

## Training roadmap introduced on Day 1

This was the outline presented at the start of training. It records the plan at that point; actual coverage belongs in each week's learning record.

| Week | Planned focus |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Business induction, professional skills, Agile and Scrum, and an AI workshop. |
| 2 | Data concepts, Excel and SQL. |
| 3 | Core Python, with JSON and APIs introduced as related topics. |
| 4 | Python for data, notebooks, cleaning, transformation and analysis. |
| 5 | NoSQL, MongoDB and ETL pipelines. |
| 6 | Cloud computing using AWS. |
| 7 | Data platforms, Databricks and PySpark, with further topics dependent on progress and available time. |
| 8 | A final project bringing the learning together and a final readiness review. |

Git and GitHub were also introduced as tools for maintaining and sharing work. The overview connected the later project work to a cloud-hosted data pipeline.

## Review questions and follow-up

1. How do delivery, engagement, professionalism and communication show up in a piece of work?
2. When should I clarify a brief immediately, and when should I investigate a problem first?
3. How would I explain the T-shaped professional without relying on a list of software names?
4. How would I change a project explanation for a technical colleague versus a business stakeholder?
5. What is the difference between ETL and ELT?
6. Which data role interests me most, and what evidence supports my current skills?
