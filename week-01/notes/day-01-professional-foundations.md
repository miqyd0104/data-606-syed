## Starting the training

The first day covered how the training will work, what is expected of us as junior consultants and the skills we need before working with a client. There was also a group discussion about the T-shaped professional and a presentation task to prepare for the next day.

The training is about being able to do the work and explain it clearly. Technical knowledge matters, but so do reliability, communication and how we work with other people.

## What we are assessed on

There are four main areas:

- **Delivery:** finishing work on time and to the standard required.
- **Engagement:** getting involved, asking questions, answering questions and contributing to discussions.
- **Professionalism:** being punctual, respectful and prepared, and letting people know about problems early.
- **Communication:** explaining ideas clearly, both verbally and in writing, with the right level of detail for the audience.

Tests are only one part of this. The work we do each day and how we approach it also matter. If we receive feedback, we need to apply it to the next piece of work.

For remote training, this means joining calls on time, being contactable during working hours and treating the day as a working day. We also discussed having a professional profile picture and dressing appropriately for meetings.

## Asking for help — three before me

Before asking the trainer to solve a problem:

1. Try to work through it myself.
2. Look for an explanation or solution using documentation and other resources.
3. Ask a colleague for help.

If I am still stuck, explain what I am trying to do, what went wrong and what I have already tried. This makes it easier for someone to help with the actual problem.

If the instructions themselves are unclear, ask for clarification straight away. There is no benefit in spending time solving the wrong problem.

## Communication and teamwork

We discussed what “effective communication” actually means. It should be clear, relevant and concise, while still including the information the other person needs. Being concise does not mean leaving out important details.

The audience matters. A technical colleague may need to understand how something works, while a business stakeholder may be more interested in the result and what it means for their decision.

Presentations do not always need slides. A diagram, demonstration or walkthrough of a GitHub README can work too. Choose the format that makes the explanation easiest to follow.

Slack is used for cohort messages and the task board, while Teams is used for calls. The task board keeps the work and supporting resources in one place.

### Stand-ups

A stand-up is a short team update covering:

- What I have done since the last update.
- What I am working on next.
- Anything blocking me or any help I need.

Longer discussions can happen afterwards. The point is to keep everyone informed without turning the update into a full problem-solving meeting.

## The T-shaped professional

The top of the T represents broad skills that help us work with other people. The vertical part represents depth in a specialist area. For a data professional, both are needed: technical ability is useful when it can be applied and communicated within a team.

In our groups, we had to choose four broad skills and four role-specific skills, then explain why we had chosen them.

I presented our group's suggestions. For the broad skills, we chose effective communication, problem solving, continuous learning, and collaboration and teamwork. For the technical skills, we suggested Python, SQL, Excel and cloud. I also mentioned presentation skills, which were included in the broader skills discussion.

After comparing the groups' ideas, we settled on these broad skills:

- **Effective communication:** getting the point across clearly and adapting the explanation to the audience.
- **Collaboration:** working with others towards a shared result and coordinating tasks.
- **Proactive problem solving:** taking initiative to investigate an issue and work towards a solution.
- **Adaptability:** being willing to learn and adjust when tools, requirements or ways of working change.

The four technical areas were:

- **Programming proficiency:** using code to process data, automate tasks and express logic.
- **Query languages:** retrieving and working with data, for example using SQL.
- **Cloud computing:** working with data and infrastructure beyond a local machine.
- **Fundamental data concepts:** understanding data quality, sources, types, formats, modelling and visualisation.

### Concepts over tools

A point that came up throughout the discussion was to focus on the skill behind the tool. Python is an example of a programming language, but the wider skill is being able to program. The same applies to visualisation: Excel, Tableau and Power BI are tools, while the underlying skill is presenting data in a way that helps someone understand it.

I suggested Tableau and Excel during the visualisation discussion. Choosing a chart should start with what we want to explain and who will be reading it.

Understanding the concepts should make it easier to move between tools instead of becoming dependent on one particular technology.

## Data roles

We looked at three main areas:

| Role | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| Data engineer | Building pipelines and preparing, moving and managing data so it can be used. |
| Data analyst | Exploring data, answering questions and communicating findings through reports and visualisations. |
| Data scientist | Using statistics and modelling, including predictive analysis and machine learning. |

There is overlap between the roles, and responsibilities vary between teams. The course has a strong data engineering focus, with analysis and some data science topics included.

### ETL and ELT

Data engineering involves getting data to the right place in the right form. It is not enough to move it if it is unsuitable for the next person or system using it.

- **ETL — Extract, Transform, Load:** take data from a source, clean or change it, then load it into the destination.
- **ELT — Extract, Load, Transform:** take data from a source, load it into the destination environment, then transform it there.

These were introduced as concepts we will use later with Python, APIs, databases and cloud services.
