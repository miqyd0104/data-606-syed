# AI Fundamentals

[← Training notes](../README.md)

Notes for the five AI Fundamentals classes alongside the Data 606 training programme. The course moves from understanding models to prompting them, designing automated workflows and delegating work to agents.

The notes follow the same approach as the weekly training notes: explanations in plain English, practical examples, important distinctions and questions to check understanding. They are organised by session number because the supplied slides do not establish the dates of the classes.

## How the course fits together

| Session | Main question | What I should be able to explain afterwards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | How does AI learn? | The difference between AI, machine learning and deep learning; training versus inference. |
| 2 | How does an LLM produce an answer? | Tokens, contextual representations and next-token generation; why fluent output can still be wrong. |
| 3 | How do I use it effectively? | Clear instructions, relevant context, independent checking and proportionate tool selection. |
| 4 | How do I make a process repeatable? | Which steps belong in rules, which benefit from AI, and where human review belongs. |
| 5 | How do I delegate a goal? | What an agent may access and do, how it checks progress, and how I verify its final work. |

These are cumulative skills. An agent can use a poorly specified prompt, retrieve misleading context or execute an unreliable workflow faster; extra autonomy does not remove the earlier reliability problems.

## Coursework reference

This table records what the supplied materials ask for. It is not a record of completed submissions.

| Session | Coursework or exercise | Evidence to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Ten-question multiple-choice knowledge check | Answers and any terminology or training concepts to revisit. |
| 2 | Parameter experiment **or** analysis of an actual AI failure | Settings and outputs for the experiment, or evidence of the incident, cause, harm and mitigation. |
| 3 | Timed team briefing from the supplied project information | Briefing and review of relevance, verification, sufficient context and exclusion of sensitive material. |
| 4 | Working workflow with an automated action, an AI step and appropriate oversight | Reasoning, a screenshot and a screen recording of two runs. |
| 5 | Business or coding task delegated to an agent | Setup, plan and adjustments, recorded intervention points, final artifact, independent verification, and cost/risk/ownership analysis. |

See the individual session notes for the fuller requirements and suggested ways to prepare. The project packs, demo source code, workbooks and actual submission results are not included in the supplied slide files.

## Reading these notes

The slide decks and specification are the course sources. Additional examples are labelled as illustrations or practice; they do not claim that a classroom demonstration or assessment was completed. Clarifications distinguish a teaching simplification from a rule that applies to every model or tool.

Product names and comparisons in the slides describe course examples. Available features, model versions, prices and organisational approvals can change, so select a tool against the actual task and current requirements rather than memorising a product ranking.

The recurring principle is to delegate useful work while keeping responsibility for the result. The **4Ds** provide a way to apply that principle: decide what to delegate, describe it clearly, judge what comes back, and act responsibly.
